`error(type, handler)` handles the exceptions of a type and of its subtypes, and `status(status, handler)` the responses of a status, like the `@Error` methods of a controller. `errorAsync` and `statusAsync` are the variants whose handlers return a `CompletionStage`. Where they are declared decides which routes they answer for:

* declared on the builder of an `HttpRoutes` bean, they are *global*, like `@Error(global = true)`: they answer for the controllers and the handler routes of the application;
* declared in a group, they are *local* to the routes of the group and of its nested groups, like the `@Error` methods of a controller that are not global.

snippet::io.micronaut.docs.server.functional.ErrorRoutes[tags="imports,clazz", indent=0, title="Error and status routes"]

<1> A global error route.
<2> A global status route, which also answers the requests no route matches.
<3> An error route local to the routes of the group.
<4> An asynchronous error route, local to the group too.
<5> A route outside of the group: the error routes of the group do not answer for it, and its `IllegalArgumentException` is a `500`.

The error route of a failed handler route is looked up:

. in the local error routes of the bean class, when the route <<functionalRoutesAnnotations, implements>> a bean method;
. in the innermost group of the route, then in the groups around it, then in the groups of the locator routes that located it;
. among the global error routes, functional ones and `@Error(global = true)` methods.

Within each level, the error route of the closest exception type answers, so a group's error route for a supertype wins over a global one for the exact type. Status routes are looked up in the same order, for a response of that status or an api:http.exceptions.HttpStatusException[] of a route. A request that no route matched, e.g. a `404` or a `405` under the prefix of a group, is answered by the global error and status routes only.

The response filters of the route and of its groups filter the response of the error route. An error route receives the request as an `HttpRequest`, and it runs with the propagated context of the filters. An error route that fails is answered with the default error response.
