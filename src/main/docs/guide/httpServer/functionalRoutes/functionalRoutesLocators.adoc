A locator route routes the requests under a prefix to the routes of a target located at runtime, like a JAX-RS sub-resource locator. The routes of the target are declared by an api:web.router.builder.LocatedRoutes[], whose URIs are relative to the prefix and whose handlers receive the located target.

snippet::io.micronaut.docs.server.functional.LocatorRoutes[tags="imports,clazz", indent=0, title="Locator routes"]

<1> One `LocatedRoutes` instance for the routes of every located shop, kept in a field: the router declares its routes once, when the first shop is located, and every locator route that returns the same instance shares them.
<2> `locate(prefix, locator, routes)`: the locator finds the target of the request, here from a path variable of the prefix. `GET /shops/north/items/1` is answered by the route `/items/{index}` of the located routes, with the path variables of the prefix and of the route.
<3> `locateAsync` takes a locator that returns a `CompletionStage` of the target, e.g. loaded from a database: the request is matched with the located routes when the stage completes, without blocking the thread that matched it.
<4> `locate(prefix, locator, routesOf)`: a function selects the `LocatedRoutes` of each target, e.g. by its type or its state.
<5> `targetType()` is the type of the located targets: the handlers receive the target of that type, and a located target of another type fails the request.
<6> `routes(...)` declares the routes of a located shop on a api:web.router.builder.LocatedHttpRouteBuilder[], relative to the prefix. Its route methods, such as `GET(uri)`, return a api:web.router.builder.LocatedHttpRouteSpec[], whose terminals, and those of its body stages, also take a handler that receives the target, e.g. `(request, pathVariables, shop) -> ...`. A route at `/` is at the prefix itself. Its groups, declared with `group(...)` or `path(...)`, are of type api:web.router.builder.LocatedHttpRouteGroup[]: their routes and locators receive the target too, and their error and status routes are local to their routes.
<7> The handlers that do not take the target as an argument, e.g. the shortcuts such as `GET(uri, handler)`, read it from their path variables with `LocatedRoutes.locatedTarget(pathVariables, type)`.

A locator that returns `null`, or a stage that completes with `null`, is a `404`. A locator that throws, or a failed stage, is handled by the error routes, those of the groups of the locator route first. When the located routes have no route for the rest of the path, the request is answered like a request that no route matches: `404`, or `405`, `415` or `406` if a located route matches the path with another method or media type. No other route of the application is tried.

The filters of the matched located route apply, after the filters of the groups of the locator route, and the server filters apply to the full path. A located route inherits from the groups enclosing its locator route what a route declared in those groups has, and its own settings, and those of the groups of its `LocatedRoutes`, take precedence: the annotations given with `annotate(...)`, so a filter bound to a `@FilterMatcher` annotation of such a group runs for it and the `@Produces`, `@Consumes`, `@Status` and `@ExecuteOn` of the group apply to it; the attributes; and the media types and the executor, given with `consumes(...)`, `produces(...)`, `executeOn(...)` or `nonBlocking()`, including when the request is matched by its media types. Located routes may declare locator routes of their own, which locate again from the rest of the path, and error routes in their groups. They cannot declare global error or status routes or ports, and have no `serverFilter`: those belong to the application routes, and declaring a global error or status route or a port fails when the routes are declared. Routes that fail to declare fail every request routed to them, and are declared again for the next one. The URI template of a request routed to a located route, e.g. the `uri` of the HTTP server metrics, is the template of the route under the prefixes of its locator routes, such as `/shops/{shop}/items/{index}`.

A synchronous locator runs while the request is matched, on the thread that matches it, typically an event loop thread, and must not block. The target is located once per request and kept for the rest of the request: a locator, synchronous or asynchronous, runs at most once per request, also when the request is answered with a `405` whose allowed methods are those of the located routes. A request with a custom HTTP method, such as `PROPFIND`, is located like any other: it is answered by the located route of that method, or by a located route declared with `any(...)`, and otherwise with a `405` whose allowed methods are those of the located routes. The constraints of a located route see the path variables of the prefixes, as its handler does.

The server waits for the stage of an asynchronous locator without blocking. When the client goes away before the stage completes, the Netty server stops waiting for it, when the connection closes or the HTTP/2 stream is reset, without cancelling it: a locator may return a stage it shares with other requests, e.g. of a cache. A CORS preflight request for a path under the prefix of an asynchronous locator is answered once the locator has located its target, with the methods of its located routes, before the filters that run after the routes are matched.
